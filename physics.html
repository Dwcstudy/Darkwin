Mechanics

Motion and forces

Use a free-body diagram to identify all forces acting on a system. Apply Newton’s laws consistently in the chosen direction.

Key formulaF = ma    p = mv    W = Fd cos θ
  • Resolve vectors into perpendicular components.
  • Conservation of momentum applies when net external force is zero.
  • Mechanical energy is conserved only when non-conservative forces do no work.
02

Electrodynamics

Current, fields, and circuits

Electric current is the rate of charge flow. In a circuit, use Ohm’s law and Kirchhoff’s rules to relate voltage, current, and resistance.

Key formulasV = IR    P = VI    F = qvB sin θ
  • The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum leaving it.
  • Capacitors store energy in the electric field.
  • Induced emf opposes the change that produces it.
03

Waves & Optics

Wave behavior and image formation

Wave properties can be explained using wavelength, frequency, speed, amplitude, reflection, refraction, and interference.

Key formulasv = fλ    n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂    1/f = 1/v + 1/u
  • Frequency remains unchanged when a wave enters a new medium.
  • Use sign conventions carefully in lens and mirror calculations.
  • Constructive interference occurs at path differences of integer wavelengths.
04

Modern Physics

Atoms, photons, and relativity

Modern physics connects particle behavior with quantized energy, nuclear processes, and relativistic motion.

Key formulasE = hf    λ = h/p    E = mc²
  • Photoelectric emission depends on photon frequency and work function.
  • Radioactive decay follows an exponential law.
  • At high speeds, measured length and time depend on the observer’s frame.
