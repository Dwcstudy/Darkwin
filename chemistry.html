Atomic Structure

Atoms and electron arrangement

Atoms contain protons and neutrons in a nucleus, with electrons arranged in orbitals described by quantum numbers.

Key formulasE = −13.6Z²/n² eV    N = 2n²    c = λν
  • The principal quantum number identifies the main shell.
  • Electron configuration follows the Aufbau principle and Hund’s rule.
  • The periodic table reflects recurring electronic patterns.
02

Chemical Bonding

Bond types and molecular shape

Bonding is governed by electrostatic attraction and electron distribution. Molecular geometry determines many physical properties.

Key ideaBond formation lowers potential energy
  • Ionic bonds form through electron transfer and electrostatic attraction.
  • Covalent bonds share electron pairs and can have polarity.
  • VSEPR theory predicts shape from electron-pair repulsion.
03

Physical Chemistry

Thermodynamics, equilibrium, and kinetics

Physical chemistry relates measurable quantities to reaction direction, extent, and rate.

Key formulasΔG = ΔH − TΔS    Kₑq = [products]/[reactants]    PV = nRT
  • A negative ΔG indicates a thermodynamically favorable process.
  • Equilibrium constants depend on temperature.
  • Reaction rate generally changes with concentration and temperature.
04

Organic Chemistry

Structure, nomenclature, and reactions

Organic chemistry is built from functional groups, reaction mechanisms, stereochemistry, and carbon-chain structure.

Revision keyIdentify functional group → predict reactivity → track electron movement
  • Use IUPAC rules systematically for naming.
  • Distinguish substitution, addition, elimination, and rearrangement.
  • Check inductive, resonance, and steric effects before predicting outcome.
